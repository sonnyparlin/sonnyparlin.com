The story

It never got easier. I got better at being uncomfortable.

I walked onto the mat for the first time on September 4, 2007, in West Hartford, Connecticut. I was 34, which felt late, and fairly sure I had missed the window for something like this. I had no idea that a single class would turn into the thing that organizes the rest of my life.

Royce Gracie handed me my blue belt in 2009. Purple came in 2010, and then I moved to Florida, found a new room, and kept climbing one hard-won belt at a time: brown in 2015, black on September 3, 2017, and first degree in 2023. On July 30, 2026, Professor Robby Donofrio of Gracie Brandon, who I train under today, promoted me to second degree.

Along the way I competed at every belt from white to black, opened Gracie Bradenton in 2015 while I was still a brown belt, and spent more nights than I can count wondering whether the mats would pay for themselves. They did, eventually, mostly because the people on them kept showing up.

Nineteen years in, the thing I understand best is that jiu jitsu does not get easier. You get better at being uncomfortable. That is the whole lesson, and it is the one I try to pass on to every student who walks through our door.

I opened Gracie Bradenton in 2015 with a handful of students and a lot of mats to pay for. Eleven years later it is the only academy in the area with an authentic Gracie lineage under Royce Gracie, the friendliest room you will ever get choked in, and I run it with my partner Kate Young.

Kate is a brown belt who runs our kids and women's programs and co-owns the academy with me. We run jiu jitsu and kickboxing five days a week for kids, teens, and adults, with women-only classes and discounts for law enforcement and military.

Competition

White belt to black belt. Zero regrets.

I competed at every belt from white to black and hung up the competition rashguard in 2018. More matches than I can remember. Some of them are on my YouTube channel.

Off the mat

What matters more than jiu jitsu.

I am a Christian, and my faith comes before anything I do on a mat. It is the reason I try to run a school where nobody is put on a pedestal and everybody is welcome. It is also why I built Vantage Bible, a website and iOS app with the King James Bible narrated and explained, the meaning of every passage a tap away, and a set of features that make getting closer to the Bible much easier.

I have been married to my wife for 28 years. She has watched every promotion, every competition, and every late night of building a business, and none of it happens without her.

Our son Jake serves in the United States Army. Nothing I have earned on the mat makes me prouder than that.

When I am not teaching, I am a software engineer and a musician. I am also an amateur chess player and a member of the Kava Social Chess Club, where I lose to people half my age and enjoy every minute of it.
